01 🔬 Peptic Ulcer Disease — Big Picture
🎯 Core Theme: Antisecretory drugs are the mainstay. Histamine stimulates H⁺ secretion → increases cAMP → drives H⁺/K⁺-ATPase. Prostaglandins + somatostatin inhibit it. Everything else is downstream of this pump.

02 💙 Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs) — "-prazole"
🔑 MOA: Form a covalent disulfide bond with a cysteinyl residue in the activated proton pump → irreversibly inhibit H⁺/K⁺-ATPase → block up to 95% of gastric acid secretion. More effective than H2RAs.
Agents ★
Rx + OTC Available Omeprazole (Prilosec)
Esomeprazole (Nexium)
Lansoprazole (Prevacid)
Rx Only (oral) Dexlansoprazole (Dexilant)
Pantoprazole (Protonix)
Rabeprazole (Aciphex)
Also IV Available Esomeprazole
Pantoprazole
(injection form exists)
Pharmacokinetics ★ — Very Testable
Key PK Facts Acid-labile prodrugs
Metabolized by CYP2C19
t½ ~ 1 hour but duration 24–48 hours
Dosing Rules ★ Take 30–60 min BEFORE first meal of the day
Maximal effect not achieved until >4 days
"As needed" dosing is suboptimal
⚠️ Highest concentration of H⁺/K⁺-ATPase is AFTER prolonged fasting — that's why timing before the first meal matters most.
Indications
Primary Uses PUD (heal in 4 weeks)
GERD / heartburn (pyrosis)
Dyspepsia
Prevention of NSAID-induced ulcers
Special Indications Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome (gastrin-secreting tumors)
Severe peptic ulcer bleeding: IV loading dose → 72-hour continuous infusion
Adverse Effects & Drug Interactions ★
AEs Nausea, diarrhea, headache
Abdominal cramping, flatulence
C. difficile intestinal infection risk
Drug Interactions (via CYP2C19) ★ Diazepam ↑ levels
Phenytoin ↑ levels
Clopidogrel ↓ activation (↓ antiplatelet effect)
↓ absorption of: itraconazole, atazanavir
Rilpivirine — CONTRAINDICATED
Note: Mostly omeprazole & esomeprazole
🔑 PPIs can cause false-negative urea breath test (UBT) and fecal antigen test for H. pylori eradication confirmation — switch to H2RA before testing. Antibody detection is NOT affected.
03 💚 Histamine-2 Receptor Antagonists (H2RAs) — "-tidine"
🧩 MOA: Compete with histamine for H2 receptors on gastric parietal cells → inhibit both meal-stimulated and basal acid secretion. 2nd-line vs PPIs — slower ulcer healing (6–8 weeks vs 4 weeks).
Agents + PK
Agents ★ Cimetidine (Tagamet) — rarely used now
Famotidine (Pepcid)
Nizatidine (Axid)
PK Well-absorbed orally
Metabolized by liver, excreted by kidneys
Short t½ but duration ~12 hours
Dose adjust in renal insufficiency
Indications ★
Primary Uses (2nd-line) PUD — heals in 6–8 weeks (2nd-line)
GERD (2nd-line; consider 1st in mild, non-erosive NERD)
Dyspepsia
Specific Uses Prevention of GI bleeding in critically ill patients (PPIs preferred)
PUD for 2 weeks post-H. pylori treatment
Adverse Effects & Drug Interactions ★
AEs Generally well-tolerated
Diarrhea, headache, fatigue
Mental status changes (confusion, hallucinations, agitation) — IV use in elderly ICU w/ renal/hepatic dysfunction
Gynecomastia with cimetidine ★ (still HY!)
Drug Interactions ★ Cimetidine = CYP450 inhibitor (2C9, 2D6, 3A4)
Other H2RAs do NOT significantly inhibit these enzymes
All H2RAs ↓ absorption of drugs needing gastric acid (itraconazole, etc.)
04 💊 Antacids
⚗️ MOA: Weak bases that react with HCl → salt + water → neutralize gastric acid → ↑ stomach pH. Effective for up to 2 hours. Used as-needed for symptomatic relief (dyspepsia, GERD) — not for ulcer healing.

06 🆕 Potassium-Competitive Acid Blockers (PCABs) — "-prazan"
⚡ Novel class. Unlike PPIs (which need activation first), PCABs block both active AND resting proton pumps by competing with K⁺ on the luminal side. Faster onset (<4 hours), no need to dose before meals.
Key Details ★
Agent Vonoprazan (Voquenzna) — FDA approved 11/2023
Revaprazan, tegoprazan (not yet FDA-approved)
Suffix: "-prazan"
PK + Indications Onset <4 hours
Take with or without food
Indications: erosive esophagitis + H. pylori eradication
Reserve for severe erosions not responsive to PPIs
💡 AEs similar to PPIs (diarrhea, nausea, cramping). Long-term risk likely similar to PPIs. MOA: competes with K⁺ on luminal side of H⁺/K⁺-ATPase.

Part IV · Urinary Tract Infections (Johnson)
13 🫘 UTI Antibiotics — Oral Options
First-Line Agents for Uncomplicated Cystitis ★
Nitrofurantoin ★ MOA: Reduced by bacterial nitrofuran reductase → reactive intermediates → damage DNA, ribosomal proteins, cell wall
AEs: N/V/D, hepatitis, hemolytic anemia (↑ in G6PD deficiency), acute pneumonitis / pulmonary fibrosis (chronic use), peripheral neuropathy
CI at term (38–42 wks), labor, delivery; OK for asymptomatic bacteriuria in pregnancy
TMP-SMX ★ MOA: Sequential folate blockade — SMX inhibits dihydropteroate synthase (PABA analog) + TMP inhibits dihydrofolate reductase → ↓ tetrahydrofolate → ↓ DNA synthesis
AEs: Crystalluria, hypersensitivity, hemolytic anemia (G6PD), megaloblastic anemia, leukopenia, hyperkalemia, AIN/ARF, severe dermatologic (SJS/TEN), kernicterus
Avoid 1st trimester (neural tube) & last month/3rd trimester (kernicterus); many DIs (warfarin!)
Fosfomycin ★ MOA: PEP analog → inhibits MurA → blocks early peptidoglycan synthesis → bacterial cell death
AEs: Nausea, diarrhea, headache, vaginitis
Safe in pregnancy — effective for asymptomatic bacteriuria & acute cystitis; NOT for pyelonephritis
Pivmecillinam (Alternative) + Pregnancy Antibiotic Chart
Pivmecillinam MOA: Prodrug → mecillinam; selectively binds PBP-2 → inhibits cell wall synthesis → bacterial lysis
AEs: N/V/D, headache, urticaria, esophagitis
CI: Serious hypersensitivity; carnitine deficiency; acute porphyria; C. diff risk
Pregnancy UTI Antibiotic Rules ★ Asymptomatic bacteriuria: Beta-lactams preferred (amoxicillin-clavulanate, fosfomycin, cephalosporins)
Acute cystitis: same agents + TMP-SMX (avoid 1st tri & 3rd tri)
Pyelonephritis: preferred ceftriaxone or ampicillin + gentamicin
Nitrofurantoin: avoid at term; TMP-SMX: avoid 1st & last month

14 💧 IV Fluids — Big Picture
🎯 Core Concept: Total body water = 0.6 × body weight. ⅔ is intracellular (ICF), ⅓ is extracellular (ECF). Of the ECF, ¾ is interstitial and ¼ is plasma (intravascular). Water follows solute.
Compartment Fractions ★ — Must Memorize
CompartmentFraction of TBWFraction of ECF
ICF⅔ TBW—
ECF⅓ TBW—
Plasma (intravascular)1/12 TBW¼ ECF
Interstitial3/16 TBW¾ ECF
Hypovolemia vs Dehydration ★ Critical Distinction
Hypovolemia = loss of ECF Losing salt + water at ~1:1 (isotonic)
Causes: hemorrhage, vomiting, diarrhea, third spacing, burns
↓ plasma volume, ↓ interstitial fluid, ↓ intravascular volume
Correct with: Isotonic fluids (NS, LR, Plasmalyte)
Signs: poor perfusion (HR ↑, BP ↓, oliguria, dry mucous membranes)
Dehydration = loss of free water (ICF + ECF) Losing free water, NOT salt → HYPEROSMOLAR
↑ serum Na (sodium stays behind)
Water shifts from ICF into ECF
Causes: Diabetes insipidus, fever, osmotic diuresis, not drinking
Correct with: Hypotonic fluids (D5W, ½NS, ½NS + D5W)
Colloids vs Crystalloids ★
Colloids Large proteins → stay in vascular space
Examples: 5% albumin, 25% albumin, 6% hetastarch, 10% dextran-40
Largest plasma expansion — 4.5× volume expansion
Uses (2nd line): hemorrhagic shock, low effective arterial blood volume (cirrhosis, nephrotic)
NOT useful for ICF — do nothing for dehydration
Crystalloids Electrolytes ± sugars → distributes based on tonicity
Isotonic: stays in ECF; Hypotonic: enters ICF; Hypertonic: pulls water out of ICF
DO NOT use colloids in: Sepsis, ARDS (capillary integrity lost)
NS vs LR: LR has lactate (relies on hepatic function) + avoids hyperchloremic acidosis from NS
🔑 Volume Replacement Math: To replace 1L intravascular volume: isotonic fluids = need 4L (¼ stays intravascular). Hypotonic fluids = need ~6L ½NS. Colloids = stays in vasculature (~1:1).
Serum Sodium Correction ★
Hyponatremia — Correct SLOWLY Risk of cerebral edema if overcorrected rapidly
Max rate: ≤0.5 mmol/hr (common conservative rate)
Oral rehydration preferred; D5W if IV fluids needed
Treatment of hypernatremia: use D5W to provide free water
Normal Sodium = 135–145 mMol/L Maintenance: water 25–30 mL/kg/day
Sodium + potassium + chloride up to 1 mmol/kg/day
Glucose: 50–100 g/day

ACEi — "-prils" ★ Captopril, Lisinopril, Enalapril
MOA: Inhibit ACE → ↓ Ang II → vasodilation + ↑ bradykinin
AEs: Dry cough (bradykinin), angioedema, hyperkalemia

ARBs — "-sartans" ★ Losartan, Valsartan
MOA: Competitively antagonize AT1 receptor → Ang II can't act
AEs: Diarrhea, hypotension, renal failure, rhabdomyolysis

AEs: Dizziness, hyperkalemia, renal failure

MOA: Block V2 receptors → prevent aquaporin-2 insertion → block ADH → aquaresis (water-only diuresis)

17 ❤️ Cirrhosis, Portal HTN & Hepatic Encephalopathy
Variceal Prophylaxis ★
Primary & Secondary Prophylaxis DOC ★ Carvedilol — β₁, β₂, >α₁ antagonist
DOC for primary prophylaxis and first-line for secondary prophylaxis
Non-selective β-blockers (propranolol, nadolol) as alternatives
Mechanism of Varices ↓ venous compliance in liver → ↑ portal resistance → blood backs up → shunts into left gastric vein → gastric varices
Carvedilol reduces portal venous flow
Variceal Hemorrhage ★
Terlipressin (Preferred) ★ MOA: Vasopressin analog → agonist at V1 >> V2 receptors → mesenteric arterial vasoconstriction → ↓ portal venous flow
Preferred over octreotide due to longer half-life = sustained ↓ portal pressure
AEs: Hyponatremia, cardiac ischemia, mesenteric ischemia, respiratory failure, peripheral ischemia, hypovolemia
Octreotide (Alternative) MOA: Somatostatin analog → mesenteric arterial vasoconstriction → ↓ portal venous flow
AEs: GI disturbances
Less preferred than terlipressin (shorter half-life)
Hepatic Encephalopathy ★
🧠 Major cause: elevated ammonia → broken down in the liver (urea cycle) → any process that ↓ liver filtration (portal HTN, shunts) → ammonia builds up. Treatment goal: remove ammonia & nitrogenous waste from blood.
Lactulose — DOC ★ MOA: Forms lactic, acetic, & formic acids in colon → acidification converts NH₃ → NH₄⁺ (ammonium) → not absorbed → ↓ plasma ammonia
AEs: Flatulence, N/V
Small studies have shown superiority over neomycin but more AEs
Rifaximin (Adjunct) Broad-spectrum antibiotic with low systemic absorption → enters ammonia-producing bacteria in gut
Similar efficacy to neomycin but fewer AEs
Used in combination with lactulose for refractory HE
Gallstones — Ursodiol
Ursodiol — Treatment of Gallstones MOA: Reduces cholesterol content of bile by reducing hepatic cholesterol synthesis and secretion + increases bile flow + reduces detergent properties of bile salts
Clinical Uses: Dissolution of small (<1 cm) cholesterol gallstones; gallstone prophylaxis in patients during rapid weight loss; primary biliary cholangitis (previously primary biliary cirrhosis)
During chronic therapy, ursodiol becomes 30–50% of circulating hydrophobic bile acids (→ decreasing endogenous ones)

5-HT3 Antagonists ("-setrons") ★
💊 Ondansetron & the "-setrons"
MOA Primary: Block presynaptic serotonin receptors on the sensory vagal fibers of gut wall
Secondary: Block 5-HT3 in the CTZ and vomiting center
Significantly ↓ episodes of CINV — use in combination with dexamethasone
AEs ★ Generally well-tolerated
Constipation, headache, dizziness
QT prolongation with ondansetron — two-fold ↑ risk with high-dose, palonosetron safest for QT
QT: only POref for CINV and high-dose
Serotonergic drugs could ↑ serotonin syndrome risk
There was an ↑ use of ondansetron for pregnancy-induced nausea — possible ↑ cardiac defects in 1st trimester?
🔑 Indications: CINV (in combo w/ dexamethasone), PONV, RINV. Also effective for ANV and RINV. Chlorpromazine = only FDA-approved for hiccups.
NK1 Receptor Antagonists ("-pitants") ★
Aprepitant, Fosaprepitant, Netupitant, Rolapitant MOA: Block NK1 receptor (NK1R is the preferred receptor for substance P in the STN)
Used in combination with 5-HT3 receptors & corticosteroids for prevention of acute and delayed CINV from highly emetogenic regimens
Also approved for prevention of PONV (tradipitant only)
Motion sickness (tradipitant only)
Dopamine Antagonists, Corticosteroids & Others ★
Phenothiazines (D2 Ant.) ★ Chlorpromazine, Prochlorperazine, Promethazine
MOA: D2 + M + H1 blockade in CTZ
Chlorpromazine = only FDA-approved for hiccups
Available as suppositories (useful for severe N/V)
AEs: Sedation, extrapyramidal effects
Corticosteroids ★ Dexamethasone (methylprednisolone also used)
MOA proposed: inhibit peritumoral inflammation + ↓ prostaglandin production
Indication: Enhance efficacy of 5-HT3 antagonists in CINV (moderate-high emetogenic); prevention of PONV
Cannabinoids Dronabinol (Marinol® — delta-9-THC)
MOA: Stimulates CB1 receptors → ↓ dorsal vagal complex tone + ↓ gastric motility
Indication: CINV refractory to other antiemetics
Precautions: Euphoria, dysphoria, sedation, hallucinations, hypotension, dronabinol
Antihistamine-Anticholinergics Scopolamine → motion sickness PREVENTION (transdermal patch)
Meclizine → vertigo
Dimenhydrinate, Diphenhydramine → treatment of active motion sickness
Doxylamine + Pyridoxine → 1st-line for PINV
AEs: Dizziness, sedation, confusion, dry mouth, blurred vision
Benzodiazepines Agents: Lorazepam, Alprazolam
MOA: Relatively weak antiemetics
Indication: Useful to prevent anxiety-provoked N/V + for ANV (anticipatory nausea) in patients receiving highly emetogenic chemo
Sedation = most common AE

Part VIII · Clinical Pharmacokinetics (Steve)
20 📐 Clinical Pharmacokinetics — Ion Trapping, Vd, Dosing
Drug Movement — Ion Trapping ★
⚗️ Only NON-ionized molecules can cross lipid membranes. Ionized molecules get "trapped." Use Henderson-Hasselbalch: pH − pKa tells you the ratio of ionized to non-ionized.
Weak Acids: HA ⇌ H⁺ + A⁻ pH < pKa → more HA (non-ionized) → better absorbed
pH > pKa → more A⁻ (ionized) → poorly absorbed / trapped
Acidic drugs absorbed well in the stomach (low pH)
Weak Bases: B ⇌ BH⁺ pH < pKa → more BH⁺ (ionized) → poorly absorbed
pH > pKa → more B (non-ionized) → better absorbed
Basic drugs absorbed better in the intestine (higher pH)
Key Formulas ★ — PAY ATTENTION TO UNITS!
Volume of Distribution + Clearance Vd = Dose / Plasma concentration (Vd given for 70 kg patient)
CL = (rate of elimination) / plasma concentration
t½ = (0.693 × Vd) / CL
4 half-lives = steady state
5 half-lives = essentially eliminated
Therapeutic Dosing Formulas Css = Bioavailability × dose / (interval × clearance)
Loading dose = Vd × Css
Maintenance dose = Vd × Css × clearance
Dose_new = Dose_current × Bioavailability_current / Bioavailability_new
Kinetics Rules ★
First-Order Kinetics T½ is constant — constant percentage eliminated per unit time
Most drugs follow first-order kinetics
Steady state = 4 half-lives
Zero-Order Kinetics A constant amount eliminated over time (NOT dose-based)
Saturation kinetics — enzymes maxed out
Examples: Ethanol, phenytoin (at high doses), aspirin (at toxic doses)
Steady-State Dosing — Continuous IV vs Oral ★
Continuous IV Infusion Maintenance dose rate = CL × Css
(convert units first! e.g., ug/mL → ug/L)
Loading dose: initial dose to rapidly reach therapeutic concentration in both plasma and tissue = Vd × Css
Oral Steady State Css = (F × Dose rate) / CL
Where Dose rate = Dose / dosing interval
F = bioavailability (0–1)
Affects how quickly steady state is achieved, not what Css is (that depends on F × dose rate and CL)
🔑 Interval options for dosing interval "tau": IV = 1 hr · BID = 12 hrs · Daily = 24 hrs · TID = 8 hrs. Always double-check units before plugging into formulas — this is where exam points are lost.
