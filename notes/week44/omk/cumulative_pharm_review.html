Glaucoma & Dry Eye

— receptor logic on the ciliary body

Master the aqueous humor physiology and every drug mechanism falls out of it. Then know the two algorithms cold: the open-angle ladder and the acute angle-closure 3-drop protocol.

PLO 1 · PhysiologyAqueous Humor Receptor Logic

Five receptors, one job each. Memorize the direction of each arrow and you can derive every drug.

  • β₁ & β₂ on ciliary epithelium → Gs → ↑ cAMP → ↑ AQ production (β-blockers oppose)
  • α₂ on ciliary epithelium → Gi → ↓ cAMP → ↓ AQ production (α₂ agonists exploit)
  • M3 on ciliary muscle → contraction → opens trabecular meshwork → ↑ outflow (pilocarpine)
  • Carbonic anhydrase II → makes HCO₃⁻ that drags Na⁺/Cl⁻/H₂O into AQ → CAIs block this
  • FP receptor on ciliary muscle → matrix remodeling → ↑ uveoscleral outflow (prostaglandins)
⚡ Outflow split
Trabecular = 75–90% (pressure-dependent) · Uveoscleral = 10–25% (pressure-independent) → why prostaglandins remain effective at any IOP.

Drug Classes — IOP Lowering

Prostaglandin Analogs 1st line

— prost

latanoprost · bimatoprost ★ · travoprost · tafluprost

MOA
FP receptor agonist → ↑ uveoscleral outflow (the exam answer; trabecular increase is controversial — don't pick it)
Efficacy
↓ IOP 25–30% — most effective class
Dosing

Ophthalmic β-Blockers 2nd line

MOA
Block β₂ (~90% of ciliary β) → ↓ adenylate cyclase → ↓ AQ production
Selectivity
Betaxolol = β₁-selective → safer in asthma/COPD but less effective. A→M selective rule does not apply to eye drops.
Systemic AEs
Bradycardia (all), bronchoconstriction (non-sel — avoid in asthma/COPD), fatigue, depression, tachyphylaxis
Pearl
Timolol is in the acute angle-closure 3-drop protocol.

Open-Angle Step Ladder

  1. Start prostaglandin analog monotherapy QHS
  2. Reassess at 4–6 weeks
  3. Substantial but incomplete response → ADD timolol (keep PA)
  4. Minimal/no response → DISCONTINUE PA, switch to timolol
  5. Still not at goal → add ophthalmic CAI or α₂ agonist

🚨 Acute Angle-Closure Protocol

IOP > 40 mmHg + sudden severe pain + mid-dilated pupil. Give all simultaneously, drops 1 minute apart.

  1. timolol 0.5% — 1 drop
  2. apraclonidine 1% — 1 drop
  3. pilocarpine 2% — 1 drop
  4. acetazolamide 500 mg IV (PO if no IV)

❌ NO prostaglandins — they take 4–6 weeks.

Drugs for Diabetes

— receptor → 2nd messenger → effect

Insulin is anabolic (RTK → IRS → PI3K → GLUT4). The T2DM tree branches by mechanism: cAMP-driven incretin axis · KATP channel · AMPK · PPARγ · transporter blockade · luminal enzyme. Suffixes name the class.

LO 2 · Insulin SignalingThe Insulin Receptor

Heterotetramer (2α–2β) receptor tyrosine kinase. Insulin binds α → autophosphorylation of β-Tyr → phosphorylates IRS → branches.

Insulin binds α→ β-Tyr autoP→ IRS-P→ PI3K → AKT
PI3K → AKT (Metabolic)
  • ↑ GLUT4 translocation (muscle & adipose)
  • ↑ Glycogenesis · ↑ Glycolysis · ↑ Lipogenesis
  • ↓ Glycogenolysis · ↓ Gluconeogenesis
RAS → MAPK (Mitogenic)
  • Cell growth, proliferation
  • Gene expression changes
💡 GLUT memory hook
GLUT4 = insulin-dependent (muscle, adipose, heart). GLUT2 = insulin-independent (liver). Insulin = anabolic = build-up; reverses everything that breaks down.

LO 1 · Drug-induced HyperglycemiaDrugs That Raise BG

  • Glucocorticoids — most clinically significant; ↑ hepatic glucose production, ↓ peripheral sensitivity
  • Thiazides — hypokalemia ↓ insulin secretion
  • β-Blockers — mask hypoglycemia (tachycardia/tremor); only diaphoresis stays
  • Atypical antipsychotics — weight gain → insulin resistance (olanzapine, clozapine)
  • Oral contraceptives — modest effect
⚡ Vignette key
One elevated glucose in a patient on prednisone is not diabetes. Confirm two diagnostic criteria; check whether the offending drug explains it.

💡 Why rapid is rapid
Regular insulin forms hexamers in zinc → must dissociate before absorption. Rapid analogs are engineered (Lispro: B28-B29 swap; Aspart: Pro→Asp; Glulisine: Asn→Lys, Lys→Glu) to not hexamerize → injected as monomers → fast.
💡 Why glargine is long
Soluble at pH 4 (vial) but precipitates at pH 7.4 (body) → forms a SC depot that slowly redissolves. A21 Asn→Gly + 2 Arg added to B-chain. Mixing with neutral-pH insulin ruins this.

Corticosteroids

— HPA axis, potency, & the price of long-term use

Three categories from the adrenal cortex (GC, MC, gonadocorticoids). Synthetic GCs work via the cytoplasmic GR → nucleus → GRE → transcription. Know which ones have MC activity, which are long-acting, and what abrupt withdrawal causes.

Adrenal Insufficiency — Primary vs Secondary vs Tertiary

TypeLesionHypothalamus / CRHPituitary / ACTHCortex / CortisolHallmark
Primary (Addison's)Adrenal cortex↑↑↓Hyperpigmentation, salt craving, hyponatremia + hyperkalemia, aldosterone ↓
SecondaryPituitary↑↓↓No hyperpigmentation; pale "alabaster" skin; aldosterone normal (RAAS-controlled)
TertiaryHypothalamus / exogenous GC↓↓↓Drug-induced from chronic exogenous GC use; aldosterone normal

Acute Adrenal Crisis

A true endocrine emergency. Patients who can't make a stress response decompensate — death can occur.

Precipitants

  • Abrupt withdrawal of supraphysiologic / chronic GC (HPA suppressed) — most common
  • Undiagnosed primary AI + acute stress (infection, surgery, trauma)
  • Known AI not increasing GC during illness

Presentation

Severe weakness · abdominal/flank pain · dehydration → shock · tachycardia · N/V/fever · confusion · hypoglycemia. Hypovolemia may not respond to fluids → vasopressors.

Diagnosis

Rapid ACTH stimulation test.

Acute Crisis Treatment

  • IV hydrocortisone — first parenteral GC (interferes with ACTH stim test)
  • IV dexamethasone — alternative; does NOT interfere with ACTH stim test
  • Fluid replacement: IV NS or D5NS
  • Mineralocorticoid replacement: fludrocortisone (often unnecessary acutely — high-dose GC also hits MR)
🚨 Never abruptly stop chronic steroids
The adrenal axis is suppressed; abrupt withdrawal can precipitate crisis. Always taper.

Glucocorticoid MOA

GC + CBG (blood)→ Diffuse into cell→ Bind cytoplasmic GR
HSP falls off→ GR dimer→ Nucleus → GRE→ Transcription
Transactivation

↑ Anti-inflammatory genes: IL-10, Annexin A1, IκB

Transrepression

↓ Pro-inflammatory: IL-1, IL-2, IL-6, IL-8, COX-2, prostaglandins, TNF, IFN-γ

Mineralocorticoid MOA & ADME

Aldosterone = the main endogenous MC. Binds intracellular MR in kidney, colon, sweat glands → upregulates Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase and ENaC in distal tubule → ↑ Na⁺ & H₂O reabsorption, ↑ K⁺ excretion.

ADME of Synthetic GCs

  • A: Well absorbed PO
  • D: Wide (muscle, liver, skin, kidney)
  • M: Hepatic CYP450 · prednisone → prednisolone
  • E: Renal as urine
  • IV when fast onset needed

Drug Interactions — CYP3A is the Anchor

CYP3A Inducers

↓ steroid level → ↓ efficacy

  • nafcillin
  • rifampin
  • St. John's wort

CYP3A Inhibitors

↑ steroid level → ↑ toxicity

  • clarithromycin

Additive / Other

  • warfarin — bleeding
  • furosemide, HCTZ — ↑ K⁺ wasting
  • cipro, levo — tendon rupture
  • metformin, insulin — antagonized
  • NSAIDs / ibuprofen — GI ulcers
  • Antacids (Al, Ca, Mg) — ↓ absorption

Chronic Maintenance Therapy

  • Agents of choice: hydrocortisone or prednisone
  • Dose split: 67% morning, 33% 6–8 hours later — mimics circadian cortisol
  • Primary AI: add fludrocortisone for MC replacement (or rely on high GC dose hitting MR)
  • Counsel: never miss doses, taper if discontinuing, report illness/stress (may need stress-dose steroids)

Drug Class MOAs

β-Lactams

Penicillins (benzathine PCN G) · Cephalosporins (ceftriaxone)

MOA: Bind PBPs (transpeptidases) → block peptidoglycan cross-linking → cell wall lysis. Time-dependent killing.

Use: PCN → syphilis (all stages) · Ceftriaxone → gonorrhea DOC

AEs: Hypersensitivity (Type I anaphylaxis · serum sickness · interstitial nephritis · rash)

Tetracyclines · 30S inhibitor

doxycycline

MOA: Bind 30S ribosomal subunit → bacteriostatic ("Tetracyclines = Thirty S")

Use: Chlamydia DOC · syphilis alternative

AEs: Tooth discoloration (avoid <8 yr & pregnancy) · photosensitivity · esophageal ulcers (full glass water, stay upright)

Macrolides · 50S inhibitor

azithromycin · erythromycin

MOA: Bind 50S ribosomal subunit → bacteriostatic (azithro can be cidal at high conc)

Use: Chlamydia in pregnancy · granuloma inguinale DOC · chancroid

AEs: GI upset · QT prolongation · cholestatic hepatitis (erythromycin estolate)

Fluoroquinolones

ciprofloxacin

MOA: Inhibit DNA gyrase (topo II — Gm⁻) and topo IV (Gm⁺) → block replication; concentration-dependent killing.

Use: Chancroid alternative

AEs: Tendonitis / Achilles rupture · glucose excursions · phototoxicity · QT prolongation · CI in pregnancy & pediatrics (cartilage)

Nitroimidazoles

metronidazole · tinidazole

MOA: Prodrug → reduced under anaerobic conditions → reactive nitro radical → disrupts DNA/nucleic acid synthesis

Use: BV & trichomoniasis DOC

AEs: N/V · metallic taste · disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol (largely disproved; CDC no longer warns; Dr. Ochs will not test this)

Nucleoside Antivirals

acyclovir · valacyclovir (→ acyclovir) · famciclovir (→ penciclovir)

MOA: Viral TK phosphorylates prodrug (selective — only in infected cells) → host kinases finish to triphosphate → competitively inhibit viral DNA polymerase. Acyclovir only: chain termination (no 3′-OH).

AEs: GI/HA · IV phlebitis · crystalline nephropathy with rapid IV infusion / dehydration

Note: Palliative — virus stays latent in sacral DRG.

HPV Wart Treatments

Patient-applied: podofilox/podophyllin (antimitotic) · imiquimod (interferon/cytokines) · sinecatechins (immune mod)

Provider: cryotherapy (necrosis) · TCA/BCA 80–90% (protein coagulation) · surgical excision

Pregnancy-safe only: cryotherapy · TCA/BCA · surgery

Prevention: Gardasil — covers types causing 70% of cervical cancer + 90% of warts; vaccinate ages 11–12 (range 9–26)

Pediculicides

1st line OTC: permethrin 1% · pyrethrins + piperonyl butoxide — disrupt insect Na⁺ transport (piperonyl potentiates by inhibiting arthropod metabolism)

Alternatives: malathion (irreversible AChE-i) · ivermectin (glutamate-Cl⁻ channels — invertebrate specific)

Lindane: ⚠ second-line only · CNS toxicity · CI: pregnancy, infants, elderly, <110 lb · no longer in US

Re-eval at 9–10 days; treat sexual partners from previous month

Vignette Differentiator — Genital Lesions & Discharge

Ulcers

  • Painless chancre → syphilis
  • Painful ulcer + lymphadenopathy + T. pallidum negative → chancroid
  • Painless beefy-red ulcer → granuloma inguinale (Donovan bodies)
  • Vesicles + prodrome (tingling) → herpes

Discharge / Other

  • Fishy discharge + clue cells + pH >4.5 → BV (Amsel ≥3/4)
  • Yellow-green frothy + strawberry cervix → trichomoniasis
  • Cauliflower warts → HPV 6/11
  • Purulent urethral discharge, dysuria → gonorrhea
  • Often asymptomatic; mucopurulent cervical → chlamydia
⚠ Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction
Within 24 h of starting syphilis treatment (also Lyme) — fever, headache, myalgia from spirochete lysis. Resolves within 24 h. Counsel patients before they leave!
💡 Neonatal prophylaxis
Erythromycin ophthalmic ointment in both eyes at birth → prevents gonorrheal & chlamydial ophthalmia neonatorum.
★ · CROSS-TOPIC

Connections across the four lectures

Cumulative exams reward you for spotting the same molecule showing up in two contexts. Here are the bridges.

Glucocorticoids — the steroid that touches every lecture

  • Diabetes: #1 drug-induced hyperglycemia — ↑ hepatic glucose production, ↓ peripheral sensitivity
  • Glaucoma: systemic GCs ↑ IOP and ↑ cataract risk; not for chronic DED
  • Endocrine: abrupt withdrawal → adrenal crisis → IV hydrocortisone
  • Drug interactions: CYP3A inducers (rifampin) ↓ effect; inhibitors ↑ effect

cAMP-driven signaling — the GPCR family resemblance

All Class B GPCRs · Gαs · ↑ adenylate cyclase · ↑ cAMP · PKA:

  • GLP-1 analogs · DPP-4i (indirect) · tirzepatide (T2DM)
  • Glucagon (severe hypoglycemia, β-blocker overdose)
  • Pramlintide (amylin receptor)

Inverse direction: α₂ agonists (brimonidine for glaucoma) → Gi → ↓ cAMP → ↓ AQ production. Same logic as somatostatin's inhibition of insulin/glucagon secretion.

β-Blockers — three distinct exam contexts

  • Eye: Timolol ↓ AQ production (block β₂ on ciliary epithelium); part of acute angle-closure protocol
  • Diabetes: mask hypoglycemia symptoms (tachycardia, tremor) — only diaphoresis remains; can ↓ insulin secretion
  • BB overdose: treated with glucagon — bypasses β-receptors via its own GPCR → cAMP in cardiomyocytes

Anticholinergic side effects ripple through

  • Glaucoma: diphenhydramine, TCAs, antipsychotics, antispasmodics → block M3 → close trabecular meshwork → ↑ IOP
  • DED: anticholinergics block parasympathetic tear secretion
  • Decongestants/sympathomimetics: dilate pupil → can precipitate acute angle-closure

Carbonic anhydrase inhibition — beyond glaucoma

Acetazolamide (eye), topiramate (anti-seizure / migraine), and zonisamide all have CA-inhibitor activity. Same systemic AE pattern: hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis + renal calcium stones + paresthesias. If you see these AEs on any drug — ask whether it has CA-inhibitor activity.

QT prolongation — additive risk drugs

Stack with caution: macrolides (esp. erythromycin/clarithromycin) · fluoroquinolones (cipro) · antiarrhythmics · ondansetron · methadone. Combinations are exam triggers for arrhythmia/torsades.

Pregnancy quick reference

AvoidTetracyclines (teeth) · fluoroquinolones (cartilage) · podofilox / podophyllin / imiquimod / sinecatechins · ACE-i / ARBs · warfarin · isotretinoin
SafePenicillins (incl. desensitization for syphilis) · cephalosporins · macrolides (azithro) · acyclovir · metronidazole · permethrin · cryotherapy/TCA-BCA for warts · betamethasone for fetal lung maturation
Use insulinMost oral antidiabetics avoided in pregnancy; insulin is preferred

"Glucose-dependent" vs "glucose-independent"

A high-yield framing question. Drugs whose effect is glucose-dependent (low hypo risk): GLP-1 analogs, tirzepatide, DPP-4i, meglitinides (semi). Drugs that are glucose-independent (high hypo risk): sulfonylureas, insulin. The mechanism difference: cAMP-mediated insulin enhancement requires glucose-driven KATP closure first; sulfonylureas force KATP closure regardless.
