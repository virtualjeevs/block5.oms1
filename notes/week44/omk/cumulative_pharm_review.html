Part I · Glaucoma & Dry Eye (Dr. Ochs)
01 👁️ Aqueous Humor Physiology — IOP Receptor Logic
🗺️ Know the map: Aqueous humor is produced by the ciliary body and drains via two routes. Trabecular meshwork (75–90%) = pressure-dependent. Uveoscleral (10–25%) = pressure-independent. Every IOP drug acts on production or one of these two outflow paths.
Five Receptors — One Exam ★
β₁ & β₂ on Ciliary Epithelium Gs → ↑ cAMP → ↑ AQ production
β-blockers oppose this → ↓ AQ production
α₂ on Ciliary Epithelium Gi → ↓ cAMP → ↓ AQ production
α₂ agonists exploit this (brimonidine)
M3 on Ciliary Muscle Contraction → pulls trabecular meshwork open → ↑ trabecular outflow + miosis
Pilocarpine exploits this
Carbonic Anhydrase II Makes HCO₃⁻ → drags Na⁺/Cl⁻/H₂O into AQ
CAIs block → ↓ AQ production
FP Receptor on Ciliary Muscle Matrix remodeling → ↑ uveoscleral outflow — this is the prostaglandin mechanism. Uveoscleral outflow is pressure-independent, so PAs work even at any IOP.
Three Eye Presentations ★ — Know the Differentiator
DiseaseIOPHallmarkTreatment direction
Open-Angle Glaucoma>21 (typically 22–35)Asymptomatic; gradual painless peripheral vision lossStart prostaglandin analog
Acute Angle-Closure ★>40 (often 50–70)Sudden severe pain, halos, N/V, mid-dilated unreactive pupil3-drop protocol + IV acetazolamide
Dry Eye DiseaseNormalBurning, gritty, foreign body sensation; normal IOPArtificial tears first
⚠️ Drugs that worsen glaucoma: Anticholinergics (diphenhydramine, TCAs, antipsychotics) close trabecular meshwork. Systemic corticosteroids ↑ IOP. Sympathomimetics/decongestants dilate pupil → can precipitate acute angle-closure.
02 💧 Prostaglandins & β-Blockers — "-prost" / "-olol"
🎯 1st-line pair: Prostaglandin analogs are the most effective class (↓ IOP 25–30%) via ↑ uveoscleral outflow. β-Blockers are 2nd-line, working by ↓ AQ production. Together they cover both sides of the IOP equation.
Prostaglandin Analogs ★ — 1st Line
Agents latanoprost bimatoprost ★ travoprost tafluprost
Suffix: -prost
MOA FP receptor agonist → ↑ uveoscleral outflow
↓ IOP by 25–30% — most effective class
Dosing: once daily QHS · reassess at 4–6 weeks
Key AEs ★ Iris darkening (irreversible!)
Eyelid hyperpigmentation
Hypertrichosis of eyelashes
Conjunctival hyperemia
Clinical Pearl ★ Bimatoprost = only PA approved for hypotrichosis (Latisse).
NOT used in acute angle-closure — too slow (works over weeks).
Ophthalmic β-Blockers — 2nd Line
Agents timolol (non-selective) · betaxolol ★ (β₁-sel)
levobunolol · carteolol
MOA Block β₂ (~90% of ciliary β) → ↓ adenylate cyclase → ↓ AQ production
Betaxolol Pearl ★ β₁-selective → safer in asthma/COPD but less effective than timolol.
Timolol is in the acute angle-closure 3-drop protocol.
Systemic AEs ★ Bradycardia (all)
Bronchoconstriction (non-selective — avoid in asthma)
Fatigue · depression · tachyphylaxis
🔑 The exam question: open-angle, IOP 28, untreated → start prostaglandin analog. Brown iris from latanoprost = expected, do not stop. Never start PAs in acute angle-closure.
03 ⚗️ CAIs · α₂ Agonists · Cholinergics · ROCK Inhibitor — "-zolamide"
🧩 The supporting cast: Three classes ↓ production (CAIs, α₂ agonists) or ↑ trabecular outflow (cholinergics, ROCK). Cholinergics and systemic CAIs are key for acute angle-closure. α₂ agonists are adjuncts with the most local allergic reactions of any glaucoma drop.
Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitors ★ — Acute Angle-Closure Key
Agents Topical: dorzolamide · brinzolamide · suffix: -zolamide
Systemic: acetazolamide 500 mg IV · methazolamide PO
MOA Block CA II in ciliary epithelium → ↓ HCO₃⁻ → ↓ Na⁺/Cl⁻/H₂O secretion → ↓ AQ production
Topical = open-angle adjunct
Acetazolamide IV = acute angle-closure emergency
Systemic AEs ★ — Cross-Links Hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis
Renal calcium stones
Paresthesias · drowsiness
Topiramate has CA-inhibitor activity → same AE pattern
Sulfa Cross-Reactivity Both dorzolamide and brinzolamide are sulfonamide-derived.
Use caution in documented sulfa allergy — risk is low but screen.
α₂ Agonists — Adjunct / Acute
Apraclonidine Prophylaxis for IOP spike post-laser surgery
In acute angle-closure 3-drop protocol
MOA: α₂ → Gi → ↓ cAMP → ↓ AQ production
Brimonidine ★ Chronic open-angle adjunct · somnolence · hypotension
⚠ Respiratory arrest in young children — contraindicated in pediatrics
Most local allergic reactions of any glaucoma class
Cholinergic (M3) Agonists — Acute Angle-Closure ★
Agents & MOA pilocarpine · carbachol
M3 → ciliary muscle contraction → pulls open trabecular meshwork → ↑ trabecular outflow + miosis
Hallmark AE ★ — High-Yield Brow ache (ciliary spasm) · miosis · blurred near vision
QID dosing → poor compliance · not used for chronic open-angle
Vuity (pilocarpine 1.25%) — FDA-approved for presbyopia
Rho Kinase (ROCK) Inhibitor
Netarsudil (Rhopressa) MOA: ROCK inhibitor → ↑ trabecular outflow · ↓ IOP ~20–25% · once daily · very expensive
AEs: hyperemia · eye pain · conjunctival hemorrhage · cornea verticillata
💡 Ca²⁺ stones + metabolic acidosis on any drug → ask if it has CA-inhibitor activity. Acetazolamide (glaucoma), dorzolamide (glaucoma), topiramate (anti-seizure/migraine), and zonisamide all share this AE pattern.
04 🔥 Treatment Algorithms & Dry Eye Disease
🎯 Two algorithms cold: The open-angle step ladder starts with a prostaglandin and adds agents if needed. The acute angle-closure protocol uses 3 drops simultaneously (timolol + apraclonidine + pilocarpine) plus IV acetazolamide — never prostaglandins.
Open-Angle Step Ladder ★
  1. Start prostaglandin analog monotherapy QHS
  2. Reassess at 4–6 weeks
  3. Substantial but incomplete response → ADD timolol (keep PA)
  4. Minimal / no response → DISCONTINUE PA, switch to timolol
  5. Still not at goal → add ophthalmic CAI or α₂ agonist
Acute Angle-Closure Protocol ★ — All Simultaneously
🚨 IOP >40 + sudden severe pain + mid-dilated unreactive pupil. Give drops 1 minute apart. No prostaglandins — they take 4–6 weeks.
  1. timolol 0.5% — 1 drop (↓ AQ production)
  2. apraclonidine 1% — 1 drop (↓ AQ production)
  3. pilocarpine 2% — 1 drop (↑ trabecular outflow, miosis)
  4. acetazolamide 500 mg IV — or PO if no IV access

Part II · Diabetes Pharmacology (Dr. Ochs)
05 💉 Insulin — RTK Signaling & Formulation Types
🔑 Insulin = anabolic via RTK: Binds α-subunit of heterotetramer RTK → β-Tyr autophosphorylation → IRS → PI3K → AKT → GLUT4 translocation, glycogen synthesis, lipogenesis. Reverses everything that catabolism does.
Insulin Receptor Signaling ★
PI3K → AKT (Metabolic Arm) ↑ GLUT4 translocation (muscle & adipose)
↑ Glycogenesis · ↑ Glycolysis · ↑ Lipogenesis
↓ Glycogenolysis · ↓ Gluconeogenesis
GLUT4 = insulin-dependent; GLUT2 = insulin-independent (liver)
RAS → MAPK (Mitogenic Arm) Cell growth · proliferation · gene expression changes

Drug-induced hyperglycemia: Glucocorticoids (most significant) · thiazides · β-blockers (mask hypo) · atypical antipsychotics

06 🧬 Incretin Axis — GLP-1 Analogs · DPP-4i · Tirzepatide · Metformin
⚡ cAMP is the 2nd messenger here: GLP-1 analogs and tirzepatide → Class B GPCRs → Gαs → ↑ cAMP → glucose-dependent insulin secretion. Low hypoglycemia risk because the pathway requires glucose-driven KATP closure first. DPP-4i inhibit the enzyme that degrades GLP-1 — same endpoint, weaker effect.
Metformin ★ — 1st Line T2DM
MOA & 2nd Messenger Inhibits Complex I of ETC → ↑ AMP:ATP → ↑ AMPK → ↓ hepatic gluconeogenesis · ↑ GLUT4 (insulin-independent) · ↑ FA oxidation
No cAMP · no Ca²⁺ · AMPK only
AEs & Avoidance ★ GI upset (take with food) · lactic acidosis (rare; pyruvate accumulates)
No hypoglycemia · weight neutral
Avoid: renal failure · hold before contrast · severe hepatic disease
GLP-1 Receptor Agonists ★ — "-glutide"
Agents semaglutide ★ (Ozempic/Wegovy/Rybelsus) · liraglutide · dulaglutide · exenatide
MOA: GLP-1R (Class B GPCR) → Gαs → ↑ cAMP → glucose-dependent insulin ↑, glucagon ↓, gastric emptying ↓, satiety ↑
Key Points ★ Hypo risk: LOW (glucose-dependent)
↑ Weight loss · N/V/D · rare pancreatitis
Oral semaglutide (Rybelsus): take fasting with 4 oz water ≥30 min before food — bioavail 0.4–1%
DPP-4 Inhibitors — "-gliptin" · Weight Neutral
Agents & MOA sitagliptin · saxagliptin · linagliptin · alogliptin
Inhibit DPP-4 enzyme → ↑ intact endogenous GLP-1/GIP → ↑ insulin (indirect cAMP)
vs. GLP-1 Analogs ★ Weaker — only physiologic ↑ in GLP-1
No weight loss · no significant gastric slowing
AEs: nasopharyngitis · URI · joint pain · rare pancreatitis
Tirzepatide ★ — Dual GIP/GLP-1 Agonist
💊 Tirzepatide (Mounjaro / Zepbound) — The Dual Agonist
MOA — cAMP from Both Receptors Binds GIP-R + GLP-1R (both Class B GPCRs, Gαs) → synergistic ↑ cAMP → ↑ insulin · ↓ glucagon · ↑ satiety · GIP-R adipose effects ↑ SC fat storage (redistributes visceral fat)
FDA Indications ★ T2DM (2022) · Obesity (2023) · OSA (2024)
~25 lb weight loss · weekly SC injection
Hypo risk: LOW (glucose-dependent)
🔑 cAMP drugs (2nd messenger = cAMP): GLP-1 analogs · tirzepatide · DPP-4i (indirect) · pramlintide · glucagon. AMPK drugs: metformin only. Ca²⁺ drugs: sulfonylureas · meglitinides. None: SGLT2i · acarbose · pioglitazone (nuclear).
07 💊 T2DM — SGLT2i · Sulfonylureas · TZD · Meglitinides · AGi · Pramlintide · Glucagon
🧩 Mechanism diversity: SGLT2i block glucose reabsorption in the kidney (transporter, no 2nd messenger). Sulfonylureas force KATP closure regardless of glucose (↑ Ca²⁺). TZD works via nuclear PPARγ. Acarbose delays carb digestion at the brush border. Each has a distinct receptor and second messenger — keep them sorted.
SGLT2 Inhibitors ★ — "-gliflozin" · HF / CKD Benefit
Agents & MOA empagliflozin ★ · dapagliflozin · canagliflozin
Block SGLT2 in proximal tubule → glucosuria → ↓ BG · ↓ BP · osmotic diuresis
No 2nd messenger — direct transporter block
AEs & Indications ★ Genital mycotic infections (glucosuria)
UTIs · rare DKA · Fournier's gangrene
1st-line in T2DM + HFrEF or CKD — independent CV/renal benefit (even before metformin)
Sulfonylureas ★ — "-glipide / -glitazone" · KATP → Ca²⁺
Agents & MOA ★ glipizide ★ · glyburide · glimepiride
Bind SUR1 on KATP channel → block K⁺ efflux → β-cell depolarization → VGCC opens → ↑ Ca²⁺ → insulin exocytosis
Hypo Risk & AEs ★ HIGH hypoglycemia risk — glucose-INDEPENDENT
Weight gain · glipizide = pancreas-selective · glyburide/glimepiride also hit cardiac SUR2
TZD · Meglitinides · α-GI · Pramlintide · Glucagon
Pioglitazone (TZD) PPARγ (nuclear) → gene transcription → ↑ insulin sensitivity
Avoid HF (fluid retention · edema · weight gain)
Onset: weeks
Meglitinides ("-glinide") repaglinide · nateglinide
Same SUR/KATP/Ca²⁺ as SU but lower affinity · skip if meal skipped
Less hypo than sulfonylureas
α-Glucosidase Inhibitors acarbose · miglitol
Block brush-border enzymes → ↓ postprandial spike · take with first bite
Flatulence · bloating · diarrhea
Pramlintide (Amylin) Amylin-R (GPCR-Gs) → cAMP → ↓ glucagon · ↓ gastric emptying · ↑ satiety
Add-on to insulin · reduce insulin dose when starting
Glucagon ★ Glucagon-R (GPCR-Gs → cAMP → PKA) → glycogenolysis + gluconeogenesis
Indications: severe hypoglycemia (unconscious) · β-blocker overdose ★ (bypasses blocked β-R via its own GPCR) · radiology
Exam Patterns ★ T2DM + HFrEF → SGLT2i first
T2DM + obesity → semaglutide/tirzepatide
T1 DKA → IV regular insulin only
Glargine ≠ mixable · NPH can mix with regular
🔑 "Glucose-dependent" = low hypo: GLP-1 analogs · tirzepatide · DPP-4i · meglitinides (semi). "Glucose-independent" = HIGH hypo: sulfonylureas · insulin. The mechanism: cAMP pathway needs glucose-driven KATP closure first; SUs force it regardless.

08 🧠 HPA Axis — Adrenal Insufficiency & Crisis
📋 Three types of adrenal insufficiency: Primary (Addison's) = adrenal destroyed → all three hormones ↓, ACTH ↑, hyperpigmentation. Secondary = pituitary lesion. Tertiary = exogenous GC suppressing HPA — the most clinically common. Know ACTH levels to locate the lesion.
Adrenal Insufficiency — Three Types ★
TypeLesionCRHACTHCortisolHallmark
Primary (Addison's)Adrenal cortex↑↑↓Hyperpigmentation ★ · salt craving · hypoNa + hyperK · aldosterone ↓
SecondaryPituitary↑↓↓No hyperpigmentation · "alabaster" skin · aldosterone normal (RAAS-controlled)
TertiaryHypothalamus / Exogenous GC↓↓↓Drug-induced ★ from chronic GC use · aldosterone normal · abrupt withdrawal = crisis
Acute Adrenal Crisis — Treatment ★
Precipitants Abrupt withdrawal of chronic GC ★ (most common)
Undiagnosed primary AI + acute stress (infection, surgery)
Known AI not up-dosing during illness
Treatment Algorithm ★ IV hydrocortisone (first-line parenteral GC)
IV dexamethasone — alternative; does NOT interfere with ACTH stim test
IV NS or D5NS · ± fludrocortisone
NEVER stop chronic steroids abruptly — always taper
⚠️ Exam pattern: Long-term prednisone, abruptly stopped → confusion + hypotension + shock = HPA suppression → tertiary AI / acute adrenal crisis → IV hydrocortisone + saline. Need to check ACTH stim test? Use dexamethasone (doesn't cross-react with the assay).

10 🚨 Corticosteroid AEs & Drug Interactions
🚨 Long-term steroids wreck every system: Hyperglycemia · osteoporosis · immunosuppression · adrenal suppression · peptic ulcers · cataracts · glaucoma · hypertension · Cushing's syndrome. The MC effects (edema, hypokalemia, hypertension) apply to drugs with significant MC potency (hydrocortisone, fludrocortisone).

12 ⚗️ Drug Class MOAs — β-Lactams · Tetracyclines · Macrolides · Nitroimidazoles · Antivirals
🔑 Ribosome targets: "Tetracyclines = Thirty S" (30S). Macrolides = 50S. β-Lactams and fluoroquinolones hit structural targets (cell wall, DNA gyrase). Antivirals require viral TK for selective activation — only infected cells convert prodrug.
Antibacterials ★
β-Lactams (PCNs + Cephalosporins) MOA: Bind PBPs (transpeptidases) → block peptidoglycan cross-linking → cell wall lysis · time-dependent killing
PCN → syphilis (all stages) · Ceftriaxone → gonorrhea DOC
AEs: Hypersensitivity (Type I anaphylaxis · serum sickness · interstitial nephritis · rash)
Tetracyclines — 30S Inhibitor ★ doxycycline · Bind 30S → bacteriostatic
Chlamydia DOC · syphilis alternative
Avoid <8 yr & pregnancy (tooth discoloration · cartilage)
Photosensitivity · esophageal ulcers (upright after, full glass water)
Macrolides — 50S Inhibitor azithromycin · erythromycin · Bind 50S → bacteriostatic (azithro = cidal at high conc)
Chlamydia in pregnancy · Granuloma inguinale DOC · Chancroid
QT prolongation ★ · cholestatic hepatitis (erythromycin estolate)
Fluoroquinolones ciprofloxacin · Inhibit DNA gyrase (topo II, Gm⁻) + topo IV (Gm⁺) → concentration-dependent killing
Chancroid alternative · avoid in pregnancy & children (cartilage)
Tendonitis / Achilles rupture ★ · QT prolongation
Nitroimidazoles metronidazole · tinidazole
Prodrug → reduced under anaerobic conditions → reactive nitro radical → DNA damage
BV & trichomoniasis DOC · metallic taste · N/V
Disulfiram-like + alcohol: CDC no longer warns / Dr. Ochs will not test
Pediculicides 1st line OTC: permethrin 1% · pyrethrins + piperonyl butoxide — disrupt insect Na⁺ transport
Alternatives: malathion (AChE-i) · ivermectin (glutamate-Cl⁻ channels)
Re-eval at 9–10 days · treat partners from previous month
Antivirals — HSV ★
Acyclovir / Valacyclovir / Famciclovir ★ acyclovir · valacyclovir (→ acyclovir) · famciclovir (→ penciclovir)
MOA: Viral TK phosphorylates prodrug (selective — only in infected cells) → host kinases complete to triphosphate → competitively inhibit viral DNA polymerase. Acyclovir only: chain termination (no 3′-OH)
AEs: GI · HA · IV phlebitis · crystalline nephropathy with rapid IV / dehydration
Note: Palliative — virus stays latent in sacral DRG forever
🔑 Ribosome mnemonic: "Buy AT 30, CCEL at 50." 30S = aminoglycosides, tetracyclines. 50S = chloramphenicol, clindamycin, erythromycin/macrolides, linezolid.

14 🦴 RANK/RANKL/OPG Axis & Calcium/Vitamin D Foundation
🗺️ The resorption-formation balance: RANKL (from osteoblasts) → RANK on osteoclast precursors → osteoclast activation = resorption. OPG = endogenous decoy that mops up RANKL. Sclerostin blocks Wnt → brakes on formation. Almost every drug targets one of these nodes. Correct Ca²⁺ + Vit D before starting any antiresorptive.

Calcium & Vitamin D — Foundation ★
Calcium Carbonate (Os-Cal, Tums) Needs gastric acid → take WITH food
Avoid concomitant PPI · Cap doses at 500–600 mg elemental Ca
Target: 1000–1200 mg/day · AEs: constipation, bloating
Calcium Citrate (Citracal) ★ Does NOT require acid → take WITHOUT food
Preferred on PPIs · post-gastric-bypass · achlorhydria
Less constipation than carbonate
Vitamin D₃ > D₂ ★ D₃ → liver (25-OH-D, the lab we measure) → kidney (1,25-OH₂-D₃ = calcitriol, active)
Target: 25-OH-D ≥ 30 ng/mL · 800–2000 IU/day
CKD 4–5: kidneys can't activate → use calcitriol directly
🔑 Diagnosis anchors: T-score ≤ −2.5 = osteoporosis. T-score −1.0 to −2.5 = osteopenia. Hip fracture carries 20% one-year mortality. #1 secondary cause = chronic glucocorticoids.
15 🛡️ Antiresorptive Agents — Bisphosphonates · Denosumab · Raloxifene · TSEC · Calcitonin
🎯 All block osteoclasts — different mechanisms: Bisphosphonates → FPPS inhibition → osteoclast apoptosis. Denosumab → RANKL mAb → mimics OPG. Raloxifene → SERM: agonist at bone, antagonist at breast/uterus. TSEC = estrogen + SERM (no progestin needed). Calcitonin = last resort (malignancy signal).
Bisphosphonates ★ — 1st Line — "-dronate"
Agents PO: alendronate ★ (Fosamax 70 mg/wk) · risedronate (Actonel 35 mg/wk or 150 mg/mo) · ibandronate (Boniva 150 mg/mo PO or 3 mg IV q3mo)
IV: zoledronic acid ★ (Reclast 5 mg IV once yearly)
MOA ★ Bind hydroxyapatite → osteoclasts internalize → inhibit FPPS (farnesyl pyrophosphate synthase) in mevalonate pathway → ↓ prenylation of small GTPases → osteoclast cytoskeletal collapse + apoptosis → ↓ resorption
Oral Administration Rules ★ Empty stomach · full glass plain water · upright ≥ 30 min (60 min for ibandronate) · no food/drink/meds for 30 min
Failure to follow → esophageal injury
Key AEs ★ Esophagitis / esophageal ulceration (oral)
ONJ (osteonecrosis jaw — ↑ risk: IV, dental procedures, cancer dose)
Atypical femoral fractures (subtrochanteric after >5 yr; thigh/groin prodrome)
Hypocalcemia · acute phase reaction (fever/myalgia within 72 h of first IV zoledronate)
CIs ★ Hypocalcemia (correct first!) · esophageal disease (achalasia, stricture) · inability to sit/stand upright · CrCl < 35 mL/min
Drug Holiday Consider after 3–5 yr oral / 3 yr IV in low-risk patients to ↓ AFF risk. Reassess BMD + FRAX before continuing.
Denosumab (Prolia) ★ — RANKL mAb
MOA & Advantage Fully human IgG2 mAb against RANKL → blocks RANK on osteoclast precursors → ↓ osteoclast formation, function, survival — mimics endogenous OPG
NOT renally cleared (mAb) → preferred when bisphosphonates CI (CrCl <35)
⚠ Rebound Effect ★ — Critical Do NOT discontinue abruptly — stopping → osteoclast surge → rapid BMD loss + multiple vertebral fractures within 6–12 months
Bridge to a bisphosphonate if stopping
CIs: hypocalcemia · pregnancy (Cat X) · latex allergy (prefilled needle cap)
Raloxifene (Evista) · TSEC (Duavee) · Calcitonin
Raloxifene ★ — SERM Agonist at bone (↓ resorption) + lipids (↓ LDL)
Antagonist at breast (chemo-prevention) + uterus (no endometrial stim) + hypothalamus (↑ hot flashes)
Key AE: VTE (DVT/PE) ★ · CI: active/prior VTE · premenopausal
TSEC (Duavee) ★ CE / bazedoxifene (0.45 mg / 20 mg daily)
CE = ER agonist (↓ resorption + relieves hot flashes)
BZA = SERM → blocks uterine ER → no progestin needed
Indication: postmenopausal woman with intact uterus + vasomotor sx + osteoporosis
Salmon Calcitonin — Last Resort ↓ Osteoclast ruffled border → ↓ resorption · also analgesic via central endorphin release → useful for acute vertebral compression fracture pain
FDA 2013: ↑ malignancy signal → 2nd-line / last resort
CI: Salmon allergy — skin test before first dose ★
⚠️ VTE risk drugs: Both raloxifene AND TSEC (estrogen component) carry VTE risk. Active or prior DVT/PE → avoid both → choose bisphosphonate or denosumab instead.
16 ⚡ Anabolic & Dual Agents — Teriparatide · Abaloparatide · Romosozumab · Decision Tree
⚡ The PTH paradox: Intermittent PTH (daily SQ) → anabolic (osteoblast > osteoclast) → builds bone. Continuous PTH (primary hyperparathyroidism) → catabolic → bone loss. Same hormone, opposite effect depending on exposure pattern. This is why teriparatide is dosed once daily.
Anabolic Agents ★ — Both Have ⚫ BBW (Osteosarcoma) · 2-Year Lifetime Cap
Teriparatide (Forteo) ★ — PTH 1-34 20 mcg SQ daily · max 2 yr lifetime
MOA: Recombinant PTH → PTH1R on osteoblasts → ↑ Wnt + IGF-1 → ↑ bone formation
⚫ BBW: osteosarcoma in rats · do not exceed 2 yr cumulative
AEs: orthostatic hypotension within 4 h · transient hypercalcemia + hypercalciuria
CIs: Paget's · prior XRT · open epiphyses · bone metastases
Abaloparatide (Tymlos) ★ — PTHrP[1-34] 80 mcg SQ daily · max 2 yr (combined with teriparatide)
MOA: Binds PTH1R but favors RG (Gs/cAMP) conformation over R0 (arrestin) → more anabolic, less hypercalcemia/hypercalciuria than teriparatide
Pearl: ↑ risk hypercalcemia / nephrolithiasis → choose abaloparatide over teriparatide
Additional CI: avoid CrCl < 30 (renally cleared)
Romosozumab (Evenity) ★ — Dual Mechanism · ⚫ BBW CV
💊 Romosozumab (Evenity) — Only Dual-Action Agent in Osteoporosis
MOA — The Dual Punch mAb against sclerostin (osteocyte glycoprotein that blocks Wnt)
↑ Wnt/β-catenin → ↑ osteoblasts → ↑ bone formation
↓ RANKL + ↑ OPG → ↓ osteoclasts → ↓ resorption
Only agent that does both simultaneously
⚫ BBW — CV Risk ★ 210 mg SQ monthly × 12 months only (effect wanes after 12 mo)
↑ MI and stroke risk
Do NOT initiate within 1 yr of MI or stroke
After 12-mo course → transition to antiresorptive to maintain gains
⚠️ CIs: hypocalcemia · MI or stroke within past year. Correct hypocalcemia before starting. Monitor Ca²⁺ closely in severe CKD.
Clinical Decision Tree ★ — Dr. Ly's "Most/Least Likely"
Renal Function ★ CrCl < 35 → avoid bisphosphonates → use denosumab (mAb, not renally cleared) or raloxifene
CrCl < 30 → also avoid abaloparatide (renally cleared)
CKD 4–5 → use calcitriol (kidneys can't activate vit D)
Allergy & Intolerance Salmon allergy → avoid calcitonin; any other class OK
Latex allergy → avoid denosumab prefilled syringe
GI intolerance / can't sit upright → switch to IV zoledronic acid or denosumab SQ
VTE History / Risk ★ Active/prior DVT/PE → avoid raloxifene AND TSEC → choose bisphosphonate or denosumab
Prolonged immobilization upcoming → hold raloxifene before elective surgery
Cardiovascular ★ MI or stroke within past year → avoid romosozumab (BBW)
After completing anabolic agent → always follow with antiresorptive to lock in BMD gains
Pregnancy (All Osteo Drugs) ★ Nearly all Cat C–X · avoid all in pregnancy
Bisphosphonates incorporate into fetal bone · denosumab Cat X (RANKL needed for fetal lymph node development)
Manage with Ca + Vit D · specialist referral
Anabolic Sequencing ★ After teriparatide / abaloparatide / romosozumab → follow with antiresorptive (bisphosphonate or denosumab) — BMD gains are lost if stopped with nothing
Don't combine anabolic + antiresorptive simultaneously (blunts anabolic effect)
🔑 PTH + Ca²⁺ warning: Teriparatide-induced hypercalcemia ↑ digoxin toxicity — monitor. Also: abrupt denosumab discontinuation = rebound fractures within 6–12 months (bridge to bisphosphonate).

★ 📋 High-Yield Summary — Know Cold
🧠 Cross-topic pattern recognition: The same mechanisms recur across lectures. cAMP → multiple systems. β-blockers appear in eye, diabetes, and as the overdose treated by glucagon. Anticholinergics worsen both glaucoma and DED. CA inhibitors link glaucoma to topiramate. Corticosteroids drive osteoporosis and worsen glaucoma.
Cross-Topic Connections ★
cAMP Family — Same 2nd Messenger GLP-1 analogs · tirzepatide · DPP-4i (indirect) · pramlintide · glucagon
α₂ agonists → Gi → ↓ cAMP (inverse direction — ↓ AQ production in eye)
Somatostatin → Gi → ↓ cAMP → ↓ insulin/glucagon secretion
β-Blockers — Three Exam Contexts Eye: timolol → ↓ AQ production (β₂ block on ciliary); in acute angle-closure protocol
Diabetes: mask hypo symptoms (tachycardia/tremor — only diaphoresis remains)

Dry Eye: anticholinergics block parasympathetic tear secretion → worsen DED
Decongestants/sympathomimetics: dilate pupil → can precipitate acute angle-closure
CA Inhibition — Beyond Glaucoma Acetazolamide (eye) · topiramate (anti-seizure/migraine) · zonisamide all inhibit CA
Shared AE pattern: hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis + renal calcium stones + paresthesias
If you see these AEs on any drug — ask if it has CA-inhibitor activity
Corticosteroids — Multi-System Cross Systemic GCs → ↑ IOP + cataracts (worsen glaucoma)
Chronic GCs → ↓ osteoblast activity → #1 secondary cause of osteoporosis
GC-treated patient → hyperglycemia (diabetic emergencies)
QT Prolongation Stack ★ Macrolides (erythro > clarithro · NOT azithro) + fluoroquinolones + antiarrhythmics + ondansetron + methadone
Combinations → torsades de pointes · always check drug combos
